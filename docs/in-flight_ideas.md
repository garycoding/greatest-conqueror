# In-flight ideas

Ideas under consideration for Greatest Conqueror, not yet decided. Each entry is a question, not a commitment.

## A northstar

The repository has no northstar (ruled 2026-11-18: neither a northstar nor its HTML twin for now). Three intents emerged whilst the requirements were settled, and are noted here as the material for one, should it be wanted later:

- **Everything progresses.** The ages, and with them the economies, the sectors of a civilization, and what a player must attend to, move from gathering by hand towards production and automation.
- **Nobody sits idle.** It is a game played together as allies: a player who loses everything receives a founder and builds again whilst an ally still stands.
- **It follows history.** The ages run from Stone to AI, the civilizations are real ones, and the economies follow how things were made (bronze from copper and tin, power from oil).

An HTML twin, if one is made, would wear the game's own look, which waits for the art style guide.

## Renovation

Engineers could turn a building into another kind in place, the player paying the new kind's cost less half the old one's, over the new kind's construction time. The question is footprints: most kinds differ in size, so a conversion would have to grow or shrink the building where it stands, which needs a placement rule of its own. Until then, demolition (requirement 28) and rebuilding reach the same result for half the old building's cost. Raised 2026-11-18.

## Architecture of each civilization

Raised 2026-11-19, when the Iron age's buildings were approved "for now": every civilization builds in the same style in an age (the Iron age's town centre is a temple with a columned porch, whether the player is Sumer, Egypt or Shang), and only the team colour tells them apart. The idea is to give each civilization its own architecture in each age, as history had it: Egypt's pylons and flat roofs, Harappa's baked brick, Shang's timber halls on rammed earth, and so on. It multiplies the buildings to draw by the number of civilizations (eleven), so it wants a plan of its own: whether every building or only the town centre and a few landmarks differ, and whether people's dress follows as well.

## Engineers repairing siege engines

Raised 2026-11-19 in the review of the Iron age: the requirements give engineers the work of operating "siege weapons and machines", and the siege engineer is the Iron age's stage of the engineer, yet engineers repair only buildings. A siege engineer could repair a damaged catapult in the field, as it repairs a wall. Not ruled; the catapult is trained like any warfighter (ruled 2026-11-18).

## Bridges of the Silicon and AI ages

Raised 2026-11-21, when each age's bridge was given the bridges of its period (log beams in the Stone age, timber beams in the Bronze, Roman stone arches in the Iron, a fortified medieval bridge with a gatehouse in the Alchemy, a cast-iron arch in the Industrial). The ages not yet built would continue the line:

- **Silicon:** a cable-stayed bridge of prestressed concrete, as the Pont de Normandie (1995): a concrete box-girder deck in two lanes, and on the middle piece an A-frame pylon from which a fan of stay cables runs down to the deck on either side. The middle piece already knows its place, as the Alchemy age's gatehouse requires.
- **AI:** a bridge in a lattice of 3D-printed steel, as the MX3D bridge in Amsterdam (2021), its form generated by software rather than drawn: a deck and balustrade grown as one continuous branching mesh, with a thin line of light along each lane's edge.

The rise in the middle and the two lanes hold for both. A suspension or cantilever bridge was considered and left aside: either needs towers or anchorages at the banks that a bridge drawn as repeating pieces cannot place.

## A redesign of the bridges

Raised 2026-11-21: the bridges of the five ages (logs, timber beams, Roman arches, the fortified bridge with its gatehouse, cast iron) were accepted for now but judged too simple and plain, and are to be redesigned later. The rules stay as they are (two tiles wide, a lane in each row, the deck rising toward the middle, each piece knowing its place); the redesign is of the drawings in `Sources/Artwork/Bridges*.swift`, with more detail and character in each age's look, shown at game size for approval before merge.
